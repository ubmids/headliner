Subomi picked C, Tour poster

C is binding (see DESIGN.md). Its three risks are addressed in the spec: poster type only for the verdict, cities and headings with calm data underneath; one pink that only means Qloo; a thumbnail that keeps both routes. The Phase 1 case for A is kept below for the record.

  1. It looks like money decisions. Promoters sign off tours on itineraries, holds sheets and settlement sheets, not dashboards. A ruled paper document says "tool you can route a tour on" to the Live Nation judge before a word is read.
  2. The verdict reads in two seconds. Red pencil through the LLM's cities and ultramarine on the cities only Qloo found is a correction mark everyone understands, including judges who never run the app.
  3. It stands out in the gallery. Most AI hackathon entries are dark gradient dashboards. A light, ruled page with one strong blue reads as a product, not a demo.
  4. One system for app, brief and assets. The printable brief is already white paper. A makes the app and the brief the same object, and the thumbnail is just the verdict at poster size.
  5. Lowest legibility risk. Dense tables at 13 to 15px on paper hold up in a 1080p video. B's dark meters look great but compress badly on YouTube; C's poster type is loud but costs density.

Direction A · recommendedAdvance sheet

Headliner's output is the document a tour manager already carries: a ruled itinerary with stop numbers, rooms and drive legs. Qloo's findings print in ink and ultramarine. The plain LLM's answer gets marked up in red pencil. No cards, no gradients: sections open with a 2px rule like a day sheet.

References: Master Tour day sheets, Prism.fm settlement sheets, Pollstar charts, Resident Advisor listings.
